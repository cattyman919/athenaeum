Set on the desert planet Arrakis, Dune tells the story of Paul Atreides, a young nobleman whose family inherits governance over the only known planetary source of the universe’s most valuable substance: melange, or "the spice." Exploring deep interactions between feudal politics, resource scarcity, ecological conservation, and religious messianism, Herbert’s masterpiece transformed speculative worldbuilding.

Complete Canonical Synopsis

When Duke Leto Atreides accepts the stewardship of the dangerous desert world of Arrakis, he steps directly into an intricate political ambush orchestrated by his ancestral rivals, the House Harkonnen, with the tacit blessing of the Padishah Emperor Shaddam IV. Arrakis is the solitary producer of the geriatric spice melange, which extends human longevity, enhances awareness, and enables Spacing Guild navigators to fold hyperspace without computing machines.

Following a devastating assault by Harkonnen troops aided by imperial Sardaukar, Paul and his mother Jessica flee into the deep desert wastes, where they are harbored by the indigenous Fremen. Through consumption of the raw spice and ancestral genetic conditioning, Paul discovers latent prescience, rising as the religious leader Muad'Dib to lead a holy war across the known universe.
